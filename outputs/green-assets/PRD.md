# Green Assets workflows

Members sign in with Google to report problems, inspect assets and review their work. Administrators register farms, ponds and solar systems, choose a zone and click inside it on OpenStreetMap to set the location.

The reporting form groups assets by category and supports filtering. The jobs page lists inspection dates. Scheduled jobs open 24 hours before local midnight on their due date. Complaints and environmental warnings can create immediate work. Members complete their current assignment before claiming another.

Inspectors mark each check Pass, Fail or Not applicable, choose Healthy or Needs inspection, and record notes. Failed checks require Needs inspection. At least one check must be applicable. Historical inspections retain their recorded condition.

The map shows a rain gauge, a water level gauge and a mosquito trap in each zone. A reading that crosses its threshold raises a heavy rain, flood or mosquito breeding alert at one of two levels. A watch means a threshold is about to be crossed, for example heavy rain in the forecast, and outlines the zone with dashes. A warning means it is crossed now and fills the zone. Each alert states the reading and the threshold that raised it, and names its level in words as well as colour. Alerts show where to look first. They are not forecasts of disease. The list view remains available when map tiles cannot load.

Alerts create inspection work. A watch on rain sends members to farms and ponds before the rain arrives. A flood warning asks for an inspection of every asset in the zone, and that zone's jobs open only when the water has receded. A mosquito breeding warning asks members to look for standing water. A job raised by an alert carries extra checks for that hazard.

For a presentation, an administrator can switch from live sources to a simulated scenario and step through it day by day. Everything it produces is marked as simulated.
