# Design decisions

- Use hosted Google OAuth so credentials stay with the identity provider. New accounts default to community access.
- Treat a date-only inspection as due at local midnight. Open claims exactly 24 hours earlier. Unscheduled reports and environmental incidents can be investigated immediately.
- Serialize local mutations with Web Locks. Check existing assignments in the same transaction that claims a job.
- Keep field data local for this demo. Browser roles guide the UI; protecting shared records requires server-side authorization.
- Keep three alerts: heavy rain, high temperature, and dengue and mosquito. The thresholds are 25 mm of rain in a day and 30 °C. Values must strictly exceed them. Every number sits in `src/domain/hazardConfig.ts`. These are illustrative thresholds for the demo and need expert review. Water level gauges, flood warnings and mosquito traps were removed to keep the story short.
- Tie each alert to one plain rule about work. Heat sends members to the zone's farms when the alert starts. Rain shows a warning while it lasts, and sends members to the zone's solar systems on the first day it is back under the threshold, when it is safe and useful to look. Dengue shows its alert at once and creates no job.
- Take dengue from a health authority feed, not from sensors. The feed answers yes or no for a zone and gives no number of cases. In this demo the feed is simulated, and the screen says so and says that the app does not detect or forecast disease.
- Start a new workspace with no jobs. The seed keeps one overdue inspection and one due today, but scheduled inspections become jobs only after a member presses Check due inspections on the jobs page. From then on the check runs by itself. The default scenario opens with five calm days. A presenter can first show a member reporting a problem by hand.
- Make a simulated scenario the default source of readings, with live weather one switch away, because a presentation cannot wait for real weather. A fixed seed generates the days, so every device and every rehearsal sees the same ones. Wet and dry days come in spells, with an occasional storm, and wet days are cooler. Every member can play the days, step through them, trigger a storm, a heatwave or a dengue report, and restart, since each browser holds its own workspace. Any member can also change the data source, since it only affects the demo in their own browser. Everything the scenario produces is marked as simulated.
- Restarting the scenario deletes every job it created, claimed and completed ones included, so each run starts clean. Problems that members reported by hand stay. Scheduled inspections nobody claimed go back to waiting for the check.
- Show several alerts in one zone side by side. The map puts one red frame round any zone that has an alert and names each alert on the zone in its own colour: blue for rain, yellow for heat, purple for dengue. Colour bands and nested frames were tried first and dropped: bands read as rain in one half of the zone, and blue marks were taken for ponds. The zone's card has one tile per hazard, lit when its alert is in effect, and counts them. Colour is never the only cue.
- The monitoring locations in each zone are illustrative positions. Active alerts use today's latest readings in the Brussels timezone, or the scenario's current day. Missing data appears as awaiting data. A lower reading clears the map alert while preserving inspection history.
- Order work with an inspection priority score per asset, out of 100: condition from the last inspection, how far into its inspection interval it is, alerts in its zone that matter to its type, and open reports from members, 25 each. One clear signal is worth about 20, which is this week. Two together reach 40, which is inspect first. Dengue is left out of the score on purpose: the score is about the asset, not about disease. It shows in the jobs list, which it sorts, and on the asset page with every part and its reason. The map is unchanged. Every number sits in `src/domain/riskConfig.ts` and is a placeholder that needs expert review.
